OAuth2 Token Service

Co-designed and shipped a production delegated-authorization service so other services can act on a user's third-party vendor connection without ever touching the vendor's tokens.

FastAPIAWS KMSAWS CDKPostgreSQLRedisOAuth2
S
Situation

A downstream research service needed to act on a user's connected third-party data vendor account (PitchBook), but no service should ever hold or pass around that vendor's raw OAuth tokens. The service also had to go from a JWT-only rework through a scattered multi-stack deployment model to something that could actually take production traffic.

T
Task

Co-design a way for other services to use a vendor connection without holding the vendor's tokens, protect the tokens the service does have to store, and consolidate the deployment into something that could pass a production-readiness gate.

A
Action

Designed and shipped a delegation-grant model: short-lived, mintable/redeemable/renewable grants that stand in for the vendor's OAuth tokens across the service boundary, with return-origin parameters enforced on every route.

  • Envelope-encrypted stored vendor tokens with a dedicated KMS customer-managed key, task-role-only decrypt grants, and a fail-closed refresh path if the key becomes unreachable.
  • Consolidated a "frozen stack + per-slug stacks" deployment model into one CDK stack per stage, then retired the legacy stack.
  • Led the security-gate hardening that unblocked production promotion — clearing dependency, secret-scan, and SBOM advisories, and stopping vendor errors from chaining into Sentry.
  • Diagnosed a vendor-specific failure mode — the vendor's refresh window is an absolute deadline from connect time, not one that slides on renewal — and shipped a renewal strategy that serves the still-valid token instead of failing the caller.
  • Found and closed a live secret leak: delegation grants were reaching Sentry on an error path. Added redaction plus a test guarding the boundary.
R
Result

The service reached production on 2026-08-05 with the research agent as its first live consumer. Vendor tokens never leave the service boundary, storage is encrypted end-to-end, and a vendor-side quirk that would otherwise intermittently fail user requests is handled transparently.
